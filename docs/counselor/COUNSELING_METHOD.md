# PAI Counselor: Counseling Method (v4)

PAI does not improvise questions. It follows established career-counseling science, keeps a
measurable model of the student, and picks each move to close the biggest gap in that model.
Everything here is generic: no country, language, institution or subject is assumed.

## 1. Frameworks we use (and what each one gives us)

| Framework | What it answers | How PAI uses it |
|---|---|---|
| Vocational identity status (Marcia; Porfeli et al.) | Is the goal really theirs and explored? Two axes: exploration (breadth and depth) and commitment | Classify the student's main goal: achieved, foreclosed, moratorium or diffused. This is the answer to "is the student sure of the goal or is it in the air?" |
| Career Decision-making Difficulties (Gati, CDDQ taxonomy) | Why can't they decide? | Diagnose the difficulty with evidence: lack of readiness (motivation, indecision, dysfunctional beliefs), lack of information (self, options, how to find out), inconsistent information (unreliable info, internal conflict, external conflict such as family) |
| Social Cognitive Career Theory (Lent, Brown, Hackett) | Can they, what do they expect, what helps or blocks them? | Per goal: self-efficacy (from what they actually did), outcome expectations (what they think it will give them), supports and barriers |
| Career Construction Interview (Savickas) | Who are they underneath the stated goal? | Story prompts instead of interrogation: role models, favourite media, a favourite story, a motto, an early memory. Used sparingly to find the real "why" |
| Motivational Interviewing (Miller and Rollnick; OARS, MITI) | How do we talk so they feel heard? | Open questions, affirmations, reflections, summaries. Reflections outnumber questions; ambivalence is explored, not argued |
| Scaling and solution-focused questions | How sure are they, measurably? | "0-10, how sure are you about this goal? Why not lower? What would make it one point higher?" |
| Interest themes (Holland RIASEC), inferred | What kind of work fits them? | Inferred from what they do and keep doing, never from a test form; feeds Mirror lanes |
| Helping skills model (Hill): exploration, insight, action | The arc of the conversation | Exploration = discovery, insight = Mirror, action = roadmaps |

## 2. The student model (stored in the Notebook, private)

For the main goal and each serious alternative:

- `identity_status`: achieved | foreclosed | moratorium | diffused, with evidence for exploration and commitment.
- `goal_source`: self-discovered | family | peers | media | need | unknown, with evidence.
- `certainty`: the student's own 0-10 rating, its reason, and what would raise it (asked once early, once after the Mirror).
- `decision_difficulties`: a list of CDDQ categories, each with evidence and status (suspected / confirmed / resolved).
- `self_efficacy`: what they actually did (claimed → tried → sustained → proven, the existing evidence levels).
- `outcome_expectations`: what they believe the goal will give them (money, status, freedom, meaning, family approval).
- `supports` and `barriers`: family, money, place, time, responsibilities.
- `interests` and `values`: themes with the behaviour that shows them.
- `tensions`: contradictions between statements, or between the student's wish and the family's wish, with evidence.

Coverage becomes "how well do we know each of these", not "how many questions were asked".

## 3. Choosing the next move (adaptive, not a script)

Each turn the Counselor looks at the student model and works on the biggest gap:

- Foreclosed (committed, little exploration, often family or media source): explore the source and the day-to-day reality of the goal; test it gently; keep the dream.
- Moratorium (exploring, not committed): help narrow; summarise options they already like; scaling question.
- Diffused (neither): broaden; story prompts (role models, what they keep doing); interests before goals.
- Achieved (explored and committed): quick verification (evidence, family, money, fallback) and move to the Mirror fast.
- Lack of information about options: give general in-domain perspective (what a path or role is like), and note factual questions for research.
- Dysfunctional belief (for example "this degree guarantees that job"): reflect it and ask what they base it on; research checks facts later.
- External conflict (family): explore each side's real concern; never take sides.

## 4. How a reply sounds (OARS)

- Each reply uses one or two moves: a reflection of meaning, connecting two things they said, an affirmation tied to a fact, short general perspective, a summary, or explaining why we ask. At most one question; some replies have none.
- Reflections outnumber questions across the conversation (MI practice target: about 2 reflections per question).
- Give before you take: at least every third reply gives the student something useful.
- A short running summary every 5-6 turns ("so far I understand ... is that right?").
- First reply sets expectations: we will understand you first, then show your picture, then routes.
- Impatient students: say in one line why it helps them, then the quick path (up to three key questions, then a first light picture). Never switch to form questions.

## 5. How we know it works (measurement, not feelings)

Per student:

- Certainty / clarity: 0-10 at the start and after the Mirror (target: clear increase, or an honest "less sure but knows why").
- Felt understood: one question after the Mirror, 1-5 ("did PAI understand you?"), plus Mirror confirmed vs edited.
- Identity status and difficulties identified with evidence (reviewed by a human on test sessions).
- Conversation quality from the export: reflections vs questions, value-giving replies, repeated questions, turns to Mirror, cost.

Across students: Mirror first-try confirmation rate, roadmap ready rate, drop-off turn, average cost.
